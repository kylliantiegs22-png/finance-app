# Finance Curriculum Plan

Nov 23, 2026 · @Kyllian

## Overview

The app teaches finance in 12 units and 96 lessons, from your own money to reading a company's accounts and valuing it. At \~10 minutes a day, one lesson a day plus reviews gets you through it in about 4 to 5 months.

- **Lesson size:** each lesson is 5 to 10 minutes: one core idea, 2 to 4 short teaching cards, a worked example, then 6 to 10 practice questions. Long topics are split across several lessons rather than made into long lessons, which also gets around the problem of AI-written lessons getting thin past 5 minutes.
- **Sequence:** Units 1 to 5 are personal finance (the money you actually manage), Units 6 to 8 are investing and markets, Units 9 to 12 are the professional layer (economics, accounting, corporate finance, valuation). Each unit lists its prerequisites so the app can lock it until those are done.
- **Unit checkpoint:** every unit ends with a checkpoint quiz that mixes in questions from earlier units, so concepts get spaced review.
- **Australian focus:** super, tax, HECS-HELP, the ASX, franking credits, CGT discount and Moneysmart tools are taught as first-class lessons, with universal concepts (compounding, diversification, NPV) taught generically.
- **Level:** beginner to solid intermediate. By the end you should be able to run your own finances, understand any investment product sold to you, read an annual report, and do a basic DCF. It roughly matches the knowledge base of CFA Level I foundations and the FPA/ASIC "Financial Literacy" frameworks, without the exam depth.

This is general education, not financial advice, and lessons should say so once in onboarding.

## Curriculum map

Twelve units of eight lessons each. A unit unlocks once its prerequisite units are complete; Units 3, 4 and 6 can be taken in any order after Unit 2.

| Unit | Focus | Prerequisites | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Money foundations | Budgeting, saving, banking | None | 8 |
| 2. The maths of money | Interest, inflation, time value | 1 | 8 |
| 3. Debt and credit | Cards, loans, HECS, credit scores | 2 | 8 |
| 4. Tax in Australia | Income tax, deductions, CGT, GST | 1 | 8 |
| 5. Superannuation | Super, contributions, retirement | 4 | 8 |
| 6. Investing fundamentals | Risk, return, diversification | 2 | 8 |
| 7. Shares, funds and markets | ASX, ETFs, bonds, property | 6 | 8 |
| 8. Big life money | Home buying, insurance, advice, scams | 3, 5 | 8 |
| 9. Economics for investors | Cycle, inflation, RBA, AUD | 6 | 8 |
| 10. Accounting and financial statements | Reading company accounts | 2 | 8 |
| 11. Corporate finance | Capital, NPV, IRR, financing | 10 | 8 |

The sequence follows the same arc as the [CFA Investment Foundations curriculum](https://www.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/docs/programs/investment-foundations-certificate/investment-foundations-certificate-curriculum.pdf) (quantitative concepts, instruments, economics, financial statements, client needs), with a personal finance front end built on [Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/) topics.

## Notes for lesson writing

Australian figures change every 1 July, so lessons should keep numbers in one data file the app reads, tagged with the financial year and source, rather than hard-coded in lesson text.

| Figure (2026-27 unless noted) | Value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resident tax brackets | Nil to $18,200; 15% to $45,000; 30% to $135,000; 37% to $190,000; 45% above | [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-rates-and-codes/tax-rates-australian-residents) |
| 2025-26 second bracket, for comparison | 16% on $18,201 to $45,000 | [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-rates-and-codes/tax-rates-australian-residents) |
| Medicare levy | 2% | [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-rates-and-codes/tax-rates-australian-residents) |
| Super guarantee | 12%, paid each payday from 1 July 2026 (Payday Super) | [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-rates-and-codes/key-superannuation-rates-and-thresholds/super-guarantee) |
| Concessional cap | $32,500 | [Rest](https://rest.com.au/why-rest/about-rest/news/super-changes-1-july-2026), consistent with the ATO's $270,830 maximum contribution base |
| Non-concessional cap | $130,000 | [Rest](https://rest.com.au/why-rest/about-rest/news/super-changes-1-july-2026) |
| Transfer balance cap | $2.1 million | [Rest](https://rest.com.au/why-rest/about-rest/news/super-changes-1-july-2026) |

Rules for writing each lesson:

1. One idea per lesson, one Australian worked example with real numbers, then practice questions that change the numbers.
2. Every fact checked against a source in the table above, with the source URL saved alongside the lesson.
3. Say "general information, not advice" in onboarding, and never recommend specific products or funds.
4. HECS-HELP thresholds, home buyer schemes, Queensland duty concessions and the Division 296 tax on super balances over $3 million changed recently or are still changing: confirm the current rules on the source site at the time the lesson is written.
5. Use Investopedia and US sources only for concepts; US tax, account types (401k, IRA) and credit scores don't apply here.

Open question: whether to add an optional crypto lesson. It's left out of the core path because the regulation is still shifting; Unit 8.8 covers crypto scams.
