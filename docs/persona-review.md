# Persona review

Five learners (Emma 16, Jake 20, Olivia 24, Liam 28, Sarah 35) play Unit 1 of Tally end to end and score it on `docs/review-scorecard.md`. Rounds are logged newest last.

**How a round is played.** `scripts/persona-playtest.py` (built on the `webapp-testing` skill) drives the **built** app at 390×844 from **cleared storage**, with CPU throttled 4× and a slow-4G network (a mid-range phone), on a faked clock across several days. Each persona has their own onboarding choices (goal, level, daily target, habit cue and time, theme), their own mistakes, and their own life: missed days (streak repair), an interrupted lesson, an offline reload, and a failed-then-passed unit check. The harness prints every screen's text to a transcript and takes screenshots; every console error, page error and failed request is recorded. The persona's plan below is written from what the transcript taught, then the app is scored.

Evidence lives in `docs/persona-evidence/round-N/` (screenshots plus a `<persona>.json` of measured facts). Transcripts are regenerated by the harness (`test-results/persona/<persona>.md`, not committed).

Severity: **blocker** (progress lost, blank screen, flow impossible) / **major** (a persona can't do something the lesson promises, or is misled) / **minor**.

### Emma, 16: pocket money and a weekend café job

Played: no coffee habit, so typed "I get home from school", 16:30, 5-minute goal; got the first question wrong on purpose; guessed on numbers; many mistakes (45).

**Strengths**
- The first screen asks a real question before anything else (`emma-welcome.png`, first question screen). She liked that being wrong cost nothing: "Not quite … Your $1,000 is still $1,000, but things cost about 5% more".
- Feedback after every answer says why. Her wrong "Priya's $15 × 52" answer returned with "$15 × 52 = $780 a year. Seeing the yearly total makes it easier to decide if it's worth it."
- A hint opens on request ("Multiply the weekly amount by 52."), and a missed question comes back in the same lesson ("ONE MORE TRY").
- The calendar reminder downloaded with her words: `After I get home from school, I do my Tally lesson`, daily, 16:30.

**Weaknesses**
- Almost nothing is her life. The examples are an adult employee: "Should I keep my savings in a bank or pay down my car loan?" (lesson 1.1 try-it), super, HECS-HELP, a $60,000 payslip, rent. There is no pocket money and no first job.
- The apply screens assume a payslip and a banking app: "Open your last payslip or banking app" (1.4). She has neither.
- She budgets weekly ($40 + $90 a fortnight) but the only conversions taught are monthly; she can't tell whether her fortnightly café pay is "$180 a month" or something else. (F1)
- The habit chips are for commuters ("I sit on the train", "my morning coffee"). (F9)
- "Not quite — less" reads as a typo on her very first screen. (F9)

**Recommendations**: add teen and student examples; say what to do with irregular casual pay; a short "your first job and tax" idea belongs in Unit 4 (noted, not marked down).

**Real-life plan (from what the app taught)**
- Income per week: $40 pocket money + $90 ÷ 2 per week = **$85** (she can do per-week maths because Omar's fortnightly example showed the method works for any pay cycle).
- Budget (lesson 1.4, but not 50/30/20, because her parents cover housing): needs $25 (phone $12, bus $8, school $5) · wants $30 · savings $30 = **$85**. She noticed that her savings share (35%) is well above the 20% rule and that's fine because needs are small.
- Goal (1.8): $680 for a laptop in 40 weeks → $680 ÷ 40 = **$17 a week**, so $30 a week gets there in **23 weeks** ($680 ÷ $30 = 22.7).
- Emergency fund (1.6): needs $25 a week × 52 ÷ 12 = $108 a month essentials; 1 month = **$108**.
- This week: write down this week's $85 and set a $30 transfer after the café shift.
- **Steps she could not do:** the $108 monthly essentials need weekly → monthly (×52 ÷ 12), which no lesson taught (she tried ×4 = $100, about 8% low, and couldn't tell which was right). Should be taught in **1.4**. She could not see whether casual pay is "take-home" (tax-free threshold: Unit 4).

### Jake, 20: uni student, shared house at $240 a week, casual hospitality

Played: "I finish my first lecture", 10:15, "I know the basics", 10-minute goal; missed Thursday and came back Friday; reloaded offline on day 4.

**Strengths**
- The forgiving streak worked as designed: Friday's Home said "Repair your streak today … You missed yesterday. Finish 2 sessions today and your 2-day streak carries on."; he did a review plus two lessons and the streak was 4 on Saturday.
- The unit check gave a useful next step when he failed: it scored him "50% — nearly there", listed the weak lessons (1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 1.7) and offered "Try the check again" (`jake-check-retry.png`); he passed on the second try.
- Offline reload worked: Home loaded and a lesson ran with no network (`jake` offline day).
- Net worth with HECS-HELP as a liability (1.2) felt like his life: "Tap the liability on Ben's list … HECS-HELP debt $18,500."

**Weaknesses**
- Rent is weekly. Every worked example is monthly, and weekly → monthly is never taught (the only pay-cycle example is one fortnightly 20% question). (F1)
- His needs are 70% of his pay. The lesson says "in a pricey rental market, needs often run higher" but not what to do about it. (F2)
- Casual shifts vary week to week; irregular pay is only mentioned for an emergency fund (Ravi, freelancer).
- "I know the basics" promises nothing now (fixed copy in validation) but still just shows the same Unit 1; no way to test out. (F10, won't-fix)

**Recommendations**: a short note on varying pay; a student-style budget example; HECS-HELP repayment belongs to Unit 3.

**Real-life plan (from what the app taught)**
- Take-home **$560 a week**. Needs (1.4 list): rent $240 + groceries $85 + transport $30 + phone $15 + study costs $25 = **$395 (70.5%)**. Wants $80. Savings **$85** (15%).
- He used the zero-based method (every dollar a job) because 50/30/20 doesn't fit when rent is this big: $560 = $395 + $80 + $85. ✔
- Emergency fund (1.6): essentials $395 a week × 52 ÷ 12 = $1,711.67 a month, so 3 months = **$5,135**. At $50 a week that is 103 weeks, so his first milestone is **one month ($1,712)**, about 34 weeks.
- Goal (1.8): laptop $1,400 in 28 weeks = **$50 a week**; he can't afford it alongside the emergency fund yet, so he picks the fund first (from lesson 1.8 "starter buffer first").
- This week: move $50 on payday into a separate savings account.
- **Steps he could not do:** converting $395 a week to a month (without ×52 ÷ 12 he used ×4 = $1,580, so the 3-month target came out at $4,740: **$395 short**); knowing what to do when needs > 50%; budgeting casual pay. Should be taught in **1.4**.

Played: no habit anchor (after a late shift, 22:00), dark mode, declined the calendar reminder (shift times change), interrupted one lesson on a night shift.

**Strengths**

**Weaknesses**

- Rotating shifts mean a fixed "after ___, I do my lesson" doesn't fit; the app has no "flexible" option. (recommendation)

**Recommendations**: a "my payslip has more lines" note in Unit 4.2; shift workers' variable pay; an "any time" habit option.

**Real-life plan (from what the app taught)**

- Goal: a $4,200 holiday in 52 weeks = **$81 a week** (about $162 a fortnight).
- This week: check her last payslip's super line against 12% (she can only check that the line exists; the 12% rule is Unit 5).

### Liam, 28: software tester, $95,000, $18,000 saved, $9,000 car loan, first home in 3 years

Played: "I sit down on the train", 07:40, "Start investing", "I know the basics", 15-minute goal, three lessons a day; offline reload on day 2.

**Strengths**
- The offset account lesson (1.7) is exactly his problem: "Loan $500,000 − offset $30,000 = interest charged on $470,000 … Interest saved ≈ $1,800 a year".
- The Financial Claims Scheme point reassured him about $18,000 in savings: "Deposits … protected up to $250,000 per account holder, per bank."
- The recap stat tiles and the "You learned" bullets gave a clear end to each lesson (`liam-recap.png`).

**Weaknesses**
- The SMART-goal examples are a holiday, an e-bike and "Tom moving out"; none is a home deposit, the one goal he has. (F3)
- His car loan sits in "savings and extra debt repayments", but there is no help deciding between paying it down and saving a deposit (that is Unit 3 and Unit 6: recommendation).
- Weekly vs monthly again: a deposit goal in weeks needs a monthly transfer figure. (F1)

**Recommendations**: a deposit example in 1.8; LMI, the First Home Guarantee and Queensland duty belong to Unit 8 (the app says "lessons coming soon" honestly).

**Real-life plan (from what the app taught)**
- Take-home $5,900 a month (payslip). Needs $3,400 (rent share $1,900, groceries $500, transport $350, car loan $420, bills $230) = 57.6%; wants $1,300 (22%); savings and debt $1,200 (20.3%).
- Emergency fund: 3 × $3,400 = **$10,200**, kept in a separate savings account. That leaves **$7,800** of his $18,000 for the deposit.
- Deposit goal: **$62,400 in 3 years (156 weeks)** → ($62,400 − $7,800) ÷ 156 = **$350 a week** (about $1,517 a month).
- Check: $1,200 a month saved is less than $1,517, so he shortens the plan: trim wants by about $317 a month, or extend the date. This used 1.4's "trim wants first" idea, which the baseline app did not state. (F2)
- **Steps he could not do:** weekly target → monthly transfer (×52 ÷ 12, **1.4**); what a deposit should be or whether to pay the car loan first (Unit 3 and Unit 8).

- The apply screen says "Planned — we'll ask how it went", but nothing ever asks. (F5)

- This week: write down the $3,510 of essentials and open a separate savings account.
